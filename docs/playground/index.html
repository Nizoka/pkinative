Playground

Everything below runs in your tab, on pkinative.js — a byte-for-byte copy of what npm run build produces, held to the build by the playground-freshness rule. There is no server, no upload and no telemetry: a certificate you paste never leaves the browser, which matters because the interesting ones are usually not public.

The siblings load their engine from a CDN. pkinative commits its copy instead, so the playground runs the build of the tree this site is published from, not the last npm release. That is the one place these playgrounds diverge from the family, and it is why they carry a freshness rule the siblings do not need.

🔑

Certificate inspector

Paste a certificate and read every field pkinative decodes: names, validity, public key, fingerprints, all twenty extension kinds, and every conformance diagnostic with the clause it cites.

Try to break them

Both pages treat a refusal as a result, not as an accident. Truncate a sample, flip an octet, feed them a private key or a JPEG: what comes back is a PkiError with its stable code, its subclass, and the byte offset where the decoder stopped. That is the error registry — 44 codes, each with a remedy, a standard and a CWE — firing on bytes you chose rather than on an example somebody wrote down. The errors guide is the whole list.

What they cannot show yet

Neither page verifies a signature or validates a chain, because pkinative 0.1 does neither. Signature verification through Web Crypto arrives in 0.3 and path validation in 0.5; both will get a playground here when they do. Until then a fingerprint proves two byte strings are the same certificate, and nothing about whether that certificate should be trusted. The comparison guide says what to use meanwhile.

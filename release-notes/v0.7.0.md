# pkinative v0.7.0

<!-- GitHub Release title: v0.7.0 — sign it, stamp it, and prove it -->

_Released 2026-09-29_

Milestone M4. pkinative could already decide whether to believe a certificate; this release decides whether to believe a **signed message** — RFC 5652 CMS SignedData, parsed, built and verified, and RFC 3161 timestamps, requested, read, verified and attached. That is the format under S/MIME, code signing and PAdES, and the timestamp is what lets a signature outlive its certificate. New exports; nothing removed or renamed. Two behaviour shifts in `verifyCertificateChain`, both in the direction of a truer report, are listed under **Downstream integration notes**.

## Highlights

- **`verifySignedData`** — every signer checked the whole way: its attributes, the agreement of its digest and signature algorithms, the content against `messageDigest`, the signature, the ESS binding to the certificate it names, then that certificate's chain. The report keeps **`intact`** (the message is what its signer signed) apart from **`valid`** (and somebody you trust signed it).
- **RFC 3161, both directions** — `createTimeStampRequest`, `parseTimeStampResponse`, `verifyTimeStampToken` against the request, the data or the imprint, and `addTimeStampToken` to attach the token without disturbing a single signed byte. With `atTimeStamp`, a signer is judged at the earliest instant a verified timestamp proves — while the TSA's own chain is judged at `at`, **never at the `genTime` it wrote itself**.
- **`createSignedData` with an `ExternalSigner`** — sign attached or detached content with a Web Crypto key, or with a key that lives in an HSM or behind a remote service and never enters the process. The attributes a verifier checks — `CMSAlgorithmProtection`, ESS `signingCertificateV2` — are written by default.
- **Conformance level L8** — the 224 NIST PKITS S/MIME messages, each verified whole: 221 intact at the CMS layer (the other three are DSA), and every scored verdict equal to the L7 verdict on its signer's own path.

## Added

- **feat(cms):** `parseSignedData` — RFC 5652 §5 SignedData with every signer, the certificate and revocation bags (RFC 5940 OCSP responses included), and the signed attributes exposed as `signedAttributesDer`, **re-tagged as the SET they were signed under** rather than the `[0]` they travel under. A recognised attribute is decoded only when it appears exactly once with one value. A degenerate SignedData with no signers parses, because a `.p7b` certificate bag is one.
- **feat(cms):** `parseTstInfo`, `parseTimeStampToken`, `parseTimeStampResponse` and `createTimeStampRequest` (RFC 3161). A TSTInfo is DER always, whatever the enclosing message was.
- **feat(crypto):** `verifySignerInfoSignature` — the CMS signature at the Web Crypto door, with the digest and signature algorithms held consistent (RFC 8933, 4056, 5753, 8419): MD5 refused, `rsaEncryption` read as PKCS#1 v1.5 over the declared digest, Ed25519 only over SHA-512.
- **feat(build):** `createSignedData`, `addUnsignedAttribute`, `addTimeStampToken`; every signing entry point, `createCertificate` and `createCertificationRequest` included, now takes a `Signer` — a `SigningKey` or an `ExternalSigner`.
- **feat(verify):** `verifySignedData` and `verifyTimeStampToken`. The TSA must hold an `extKeyUsage` that is present, critical and names `timeStamping` alone (RFC 3161 §2.3); `allowNonCriticalTimestampingEku` is the documented escape, off by default. `verifyTimeStampToken` takes the token alone or the whole `TimeStampResp`, and a response that granted nothing is a verdict, `PKI_REASON_TSP_NOT_GRANTED`, carrying the TSA's status, text and failure codes.
- **feat(types):** `PkiCmsError` with four `PKI_CMS_*` codes; four `PKI_DIAG_CMS_*` diagnostics; eleven `PKI_REASON_CMS_*` / `PKI_REASON_TSP_*` reasons; three named limits — `maxSignerInfos`, `maxCmsAttributes`, `maxCmsBagEntries`.

## Fixed

- **fix(revocation):** an OCSP response carrying a recognised extension was refused — the same defect the CRL parser shipped in 0.5, hidden until now because the extension a response usually carries is the nonce, which the certificate extension reader never decodes. Found while writing the TSTInfo reader.
- **fix(verify):** a revocation list or OCSP response passed twice was read twice, and every revocation reason reported once per copy. A signed message carries the list its signer relied on and the caller passes the one they downloaded, so this was the common case. Found by L8.
- **fix(verify):** `requireRevocation` now reaches the timestamps `verifySignedData` checks; before, a TSA nobody could check for revocation still counted as evidence for `atTimeStamp`.

## Conformance

- **L8 — the 224 NIST PKITS S/MIME messages**, each linked to its test through its own `SignerIdentifier` rather than a table of names, and verified by `verifySignedData` with L7's anchor, lists and instant and only the certificates the message carries. **221 of 224 intact at the CMS layer**; the three that are not are DSA, which Web Crypto does not implement. **204 of 204 scored verdicts equal the L7 verdict** on the signer's own path, and 195 of 204 agree with NIST (95.59 %) — the same nine deviations as L7, one of which — `InvalidDNandRFC822nameConstraintsTest29` — accepts what NIST rejects. 68 messages are pinned on their reason codes.
- **L6 and L7 unchanged**: x509-limbo 9 156 of 9 208 (99.44 %), NIST PKITS 194 of 203 (95.57 %).

## Known limitations

- **`parseSignedData` decodes the whole tree**, so a very large `.p7b` meets `maxNodes` before `maxCmsBagEntries`.
- **The RFC 4056 §3 check that RSASSA-PSS parameters in a certificate's SubjectPublicKeyInfo agree with the signer's is not made.**
- **When both ESS attributes are present, only `signingCertificateV2` is checked.**
- **ETSI long-term formats are not implemented** — no B-LTA archive timestamps, no proof of existence chained over several timestamps. `atTimeStamp` takes one level of evidence.
- **DSA and Ed448 signers are not verified**: Web Crypto implements neither DSA nor SHAKE256, and pkinative implements no signature algorithm itself.
- **Nothing here fetches** — not a CRL, not an OCSP response, not a timestamp.
- **ClusterFuzzLite has still never executed.**

## Upgrade

Additive for anyone on v0.5.0: no export was removed or renamed. Code that passed a `SigningKey` to `createCertificate` or `createCertificationRequest` compiles unchanged, since `Signer` includes it. Two reports of `verifyCertificateChain` read differently, described below; neither turns a refusal into an acceptance.

## Downstream integration notes

- **New public API.** `parseSignedData`, `verifySignerInfoSignature`, `createSignedData`, `addUnsignedAttribute`, `addTimeStampToken`, `parseTstInfo`, `parseTimeStampToken`, `parseTimeStampResponse`, `createTimeStampRequest`, `verifySignedData`, `verifyTimeStampToken`, `PkiCmsError`, and the types they refer to — `ExternalSigner` and `Signer` among them. `docs/assets/api.json` lists every export with its module: 253 now, 152 types and 101 values.
- **Widened parameters.** The signing parameter of `createCertificate` and `createCertificationRequest` is now `Signer` (`SigningKey | ExternalSigner`). An `ExternalSigner` returns what `crypto.subtle.sign` would: **raw r‖s for ECDSA, not DER** — returning DER is refused with `PKI_API_MISUSE`.
- **Behaviour shift: one list, one reason.** `verifyCertificateChain` reads each distinct CRL and OCSP response once, by encoding. A caller that counted reasons will see one `PKI_REASON_REVOKED` where it used to see one per copy of the list.
- **New error, diagnostic and reason codes** — four `PKI_CMS_*`, four `PKI_DIAG_CMS_*`, eleven `PKI_REASON_CMS_*` / `PKI_REASON_TSP_*` — are in `docs/data/errors.json`, `diagnostics.json` and `reasons.json`, each checked in both directions by `verify:docs`. Three named limits join `docs/data/limits.json`.
- **Layers.** `build` now imports `hash` (a `messageDigest` is one), and a new `cms` layer sits between `x509` and `verify`. `cms` never imports `crypto`, for the reason `path` does not: verdicts arrive precomputed, so parsing and judging stay synchronous.
- **The error vocabulary is not frozen yet.** It freezes at 0.8.0; until then a code may still be renamed in a minor release, and this note will say so when it happens.
- **Bundle cost.** The package is 226.1 KB minified. Reading a signed message ships no Web Crypto code (70.5 KB, most of it the certificate parser); the builder ships no parser (35.2 KB); the two verdicts are `verifyCertificateChain` plus the CMS layer (170.5 KB). `dist/index.d.ts` has passed the 200 000-byte mark at which subpath exports are to be reconsidered — at 1.0, for the whole partition at once.
